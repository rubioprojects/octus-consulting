Brazil's sports betting regulation came into full effect in January 2025, after years of discussion and a rushed licensing process in late 2024. For operators still trying to understand what this means in practice, here is a direct breakdown.

## What changed

The Secretaria de Prêmios e Apostas (SPA), operating under the Ministry of Finance (MF), is now the regulatory authority. Operators must hold a Brazilian license to legally accept bets from Brazilian users — offshore licenses no longer provide cover.

The license costs BRL 30 million (approximately EUR 5 million) for a five-year term. This is a flat fee, not dependent on revenue.

## The certification requirement

All platforms must be certified by a GLI-accredited laboratory before going live. This process typically takes 3–6 months and requires full platform documentation, source code review and compliance testing.

Most operators underestimate this step. The certification is not a formality — it is a technical audit.

## Corporate structure requirements

Brazilian regulation requires a locally incorporated entity. Offshore structures alone are insufficient. Operators typically use a Brazilian subsidiary alongside their offshore holding structure.

## Banking and payments

High-risk banking for Brazilian iGaming operations remains challenging. PIX is the dominant payment method for deposits and withdrawals, but PSP agreements require documented regulatory status.

## Timeline reality

From decision to operation, a realistic timeline is 12–18 months for an operator starting from scratch. For operators already holding offshore licenses with compliant platforms, 6–9 months is achievable with the right execution.
